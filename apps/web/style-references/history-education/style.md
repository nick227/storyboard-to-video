# History Education
Ken Burns documentary stills: archival photographs, period paintings, engraved maps, handwritten letters, and museum artifacts treated as the frame itself. Soft film grain, gentle vignette, restrained sepia-to-desaturated color or careful black-and-white; parchment, print texture, and aged paper edges when the source is a document.

Compose for slow study: one primary subject fills the frame with room to pan or push in—a portrait cropped at the eyes, a map detail, a signature line, a battlefield panorama, a labeled diagram of an institution. Prefer real historical media and tactile object photography over invented scenes. Light like a gallery: directional key on the artifact, quiet shadow at the edges, no neon or glossy product sheen.

No cartoon, cut-paper explainer collage, slick 3D CGI, stock handshake clichés, or modern lifestyle photography unless the beat is explicitly contemporary.
